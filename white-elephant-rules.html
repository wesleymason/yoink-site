White elephant rules

The basics, plus the house rules people argue about and how most groups settle them.

The short version: everyone brings one wrapped gift. On your turn, open a new gift or steal an opened one. A stolen-from player gets a turn right away. When the last gift is opened, the game ends. Everything else is a house rule.

The basic rules

  1. Everyone brings one wrapped gift, usually within an agreed budget, and puts it in a pile. Nobody says which is theirs.
  2. Draw a turn order. Numbers from a hat, cards, or an app. Player 1 goes first.
  3. On your turn, do one of two things: open a gift from the pile, or steal an already-opened gift from someone else.
  4. If your gift is stolen, you go right away. You can open a new gift or steal a different one. You can't steal back the gift that was just taken from you.
  5. The game ends when the last gift is opened and any steals it sets off are resolved.

The rules everyone argues about

These are the ones to settle before you start, out loud, so nobody relitigates them mid-game.

How many times can a gift be stolen?

The most common house rule is three steals and it's frozen: after the third steal, that gift stays with whoever has it. Some groups allow unlimited steals, which makes the game longer and louder. With more than about twelve people, a limit of two keeps the night moving.

Can you steal back right away?

Usually no immediate steal-backs: if someone takes your gift, you can't take it straight back on the turn you just got. You can steal it later, once someone else has had a turn. This one rule prevents the two-person ping-pong that stalls the game.

Does player 1 get another turn?

Player 1 has the worst spot: nothing to steal, and the whole game to be stolen from. Many groups give player 1 a final swap at the end: once every gift is open, player 1 can trade their gift for any unfrozen one. Whoever loses that swap doesn't get to go again.

What counts as a steal?

Taking a gift from someone is one steal against that gift. If you play with duels, settle whether an attempted steal that the challenger loses still counts toward the gift's steal limit. Counting it keeps gifts from being challenged endlessly.

Optional rules that make it more fun

  • Duels. When someone tries to steal, the two players play a thirty-second game for it: a staring contest, cup stacking, rock paper scissors. Winner keeps the gift. It turns steals into the best part of the night.
  • Themed gifts. Everything under $20, or everything from a gas station, or the gift you'd least like to receive.
  • Gag gift plus real gift. One joke gift and one good one, so even the loser leaves with something.
  • Swap sides. At the end, anyone can propose a trade; both sides have to agree.

A printable rules card

If you want one line to read out before you start: "Open or steal. Stolen from, you go next. No stealing right back. Three steals and a gift is frozen. Player 1 gets a final swap." Agree on each item, then begin.
